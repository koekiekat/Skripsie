\mysection{Background}{Background}\label{sec:background-overview}
Since the late 1990s there have been indications that whales have been struck by shipping vessels.\cite{vessel_collision_since_1990s} 
Today this has become a larger concern, as vessel speeds and traffic has substantially increased.  
In False Bay, South Africa, a colony of Bryde's whales have been detected. Bryde's whales are found to migrate in smaller regions 
instead of the usual large-scale migrations of other whale species. Due to the increase in shipping traffic as well as the fishing 
activities in the area, the Bryde's whales in this are are also in danger.\cite{bryde_whales_SA}
Detection and classification systems for whale calls can be used to monitor the migration patterns of the specific whale colony throughout 
the year. The information gained from such analysis can help determine when vessel traffic should be diverted for the safety of the whale population.
These types of systems have been implemented on different whale colonies, but the migration patterns of the False Bay colony has not yet been 
thoroughly documented.\cite{reducing_vessel_collisions} 
Due to the variations in call types between different colonies of some whale species (killer whales\cite{killer_whale_dialects}, blue 
whales\cite{blue_whale_song_variation}), models trained for other Bryde's whale colonies would not be completely trustworthy for the detection of 
calls within the False Bay Bryde's Whale colony as universiality of calls cannot be assumed. Therefore, a model trained specifically for the False 
Bay colony would be beneficial. Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) has been shown to be an effective whale classification method on different whale colonies.\cite{DTW_has_worked}
