After the final detection and analysis of the three different models an overall analysis must be done to determine which of the feature extraction methods 
was the best as well as whether Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) works overall as a detection algorithm for Bryde's Whales in Fasle Bay.

Both the 5 and 10 template sub-experiments had an average F1 score of about 0.68 and both 
chose 0.512 seconds as the best frame length. The individual F1 scores for the 5 template sub-experiment 
were less stable across the three thresholds than for the 10 template sub-experiment. This can be seen in 
Table \ref{tab:stft_calib_results}.

Considering the more consistent F1 scores for the 10 template sub-experiment, it performs 
the best in the threshold and frame length selection stage of the Short Time Fourier Transform (STFT) model.

In terms of precision, recall and F1 score, as seen in Table \ref{tab:stft_vote_results}, the 10 template sub-experiment for the STFT experiment performed the best 
when selecting the best vote fraction. The vote fraction for the 5 template sub-experiment was 0.6 and for the 10 template 
sub-experiment it was 0.4. This mean that 3 out of the 5 templates had to agree on a call for the 5 template sub-experiment 
and 4 out of 10 templates had to agree for the 10 template sub-experiment.

Overall the 10 template sub-experiment performed the best in terms of calibration of both the frame length and the ovte fraction. 
However, since both final F1-scores were above 0.9 the difference in performance was not much.

performed best with a 0.06 difference and the highest being 0.946. 

Since the precision difference between the two sub-experiments was the largest the 5 template sub-experiment 
outperformed the 10 template sub-experiment in terms of threshold calibration. This can also be seen in the final F1 
score with the highest being 0.792 for the 5 template sub-experiment. 

By looking at Figures \ref{fig:stft_5_pr} and \ref{fig:stft_10_pr} it can be seen that both sub-experiments 
performed well in terms of the selection of the hyperparameters as both marked precision and recall points are 
in the optimal position of their respective curve. Therefore despite the slightly better numerical performance both 
STFT sub-experiments performed well in terms of hyperparameter selection.

\mysection{General Observations}{General Observations}\label{sec:obs}

Considering the low availability of well labeled data to use for the calibration 
stage of this project as well as human error, which was introduced in manual 
selection of calibration and background templates, the varying performance of the 
calibrated thresholds is justified. The lack of data and human error meant that 
a maximum of 10 templates per call type could be used for calibration. It also caused 
a bias in the choices of templates to train on as selecting suitably representative c
templates per call type became difficult. The selection of background templates for 
calibration meant that the class imbalance between calls and background noise 
differed between calibration and testing. The selection of templates and not 
a continuos audio file for calibration also meant that subsequence DTW did not necessarily 
act within the same conditions as testing thus creating another hinderance. 

When looking at printed spectrograms of False Positives detected by the model 
it was found that for both experiments many of these False Positives were actually 
unlabeled calls. This means that the actual precision of the model may be higher 
than the reported amounts. This makes sense as human error was also introduced in 
call labeling, since the known labeled calls were found by previous projects and 
is therefore not necessarily a complete list.

Considering the relitively good performance of the model as well as the unlabeled 
True Positives STFT feature extraction is a good method to use for call detection with 
the DTW algorithm.
