\mychapter{Results}{Results}{}\label{chap:results}

After the final detection and seperate analysis of the three different models an overall analysis 
must be done to determine which of the feature extraction methods was the best as well 
as whether dynamic time warping (DTW) works overall as a detection algorithm for 
Bryde's Whales in False Bay.

\mysection{Experiment 1: STFT Features}{Experiment 1: STFT Features}\label{sec:exp-1-stft}
For the short time fourier transform (STFT) model used for call detection two experiments were conducted. The first 
considered the results given the use of 5 templates used as the analysis database for both hyperparameter selection and evaluation. 
The second considered the use of 10 templates, the original 5 with 5 new templates added to increase the database diversity.

\mysection{General Observations}{General Observations}\label{sec:obs}

The lack of data and human error meant that 
a maximum of 10 templates per call type could be used for call type calibration templates. It also caused 
a bias in the choices of templates to train on as selecting suitably representative
templates per call type became difficult. The selection of background calibration templates 
meant that the class imbalance between calls and background noise 
differed between hyperparameter selection and evaluation. The selection of templates and not 
a continuos audio file for hyperparameter selection also meant that subsequence dynamic time warping (DTW) did not necessarily 
act within the same conditions as evaluation thus creating another difference between the two datasets.

Considering the low availability of well labeled data to use for the hyperparameter selection 
stage of this project as well as human error, which was introduced in manual 
selection of calibration templates, the varying performance of the 
calibrated thresholds is justified. This varying performance can be seen in Tables~\ref{tab:stft_calib_results},
~\ref{tab:mfcc_calib_results} and~\ref{tab:stft_calib_results}.
