\mychapter{Results}{Results}{}\label{chap:results}

After conducting experiments for the three different models (STFT, MFCC and LPCC), analysis 
must be done to determine which of the feature extraction methods worked best for dynamic time warping (DTW). 
This analysis will be done by comparing f1-scores and average precision (AP).

\mysection{Experiment 1: STFT Features}{Experiment 1: STFT Features}\label{sec:exp-1-stft}
For the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) model used for call detection, two experiments were conducted. The first 
considered the results given the use of 5 templates per call type as the analysis database for both hyperparameter selection and evaluation. 
The second considered the use of 10 templates per call type, the original 5 with 5 new templates added to increase the database diversity.

The similar average f1-scores for the two experiments show that the diversification of templates does not have an effect on threshold selection. 
The lowest f1-scores point to the possible effect of multi-tones often being mixed with 
both single-tones and burst-tonals because of human error incorporated by different labellers.

\mysection{Experiment 2: MFCC Features}{Experiment 2: MFCC Features}\label{sec:exp-2-mfcc}

with no added derivatives for the feature. The second experiment used the original 13 feature coefficients from experiment 1 and added 13 more 
coefficients in the form of the derivatives of the original 13. The third and final experiment considered the 26 coefficients used in the second 
experiment and added another 13 in the form of the second derivatives of the original 13 MFCC output coefficients.

For each experiment, two sub-experiments were performed. The first was performed using 5 templates and the second using 10 templates.

the 5- and 10-template sub-experiments. These values were 0.384 and 0.256 seconds for the 5- and 10-template

For all three experiments the 5-template sub-experiment achieved a higher 
average f1-score over all three thresholds. In terms of average f1-score over

third experiment containing all 39 coefficients. This may be linked to the simplicity of the call type, as it only contains

the single-tone call type and the other two call types was rather large, further supporting this argument.

\mysection{General Observations}{General Observations}\label{sec:obs}

The lack of data and human error meant that 
a maximum of 10 templates per call type could be used for call type calibration templates. It also caused 
a bias in the choice of templates to train on, as selecting suitably representative
templates per call type became difficult. The selection of background calibration templates 
meant that the class imbalance between calls and background noise 
differed between hyperparameter selection and evaluation. The selection of templates, and not 
a continuous audio file, for hyperparameter selection also meant that subsequence DTW did not necessarily 
act under the same conditions as in evaluation, thus creating another difference between the two datasets.

The varying performance of the calibrated thresholds is consistent with the low availability of well-labelled data to use 
for the hyperparameter selection stage of this project, as well as human error, which was introduced in the manual 
selection of calibration templates. This varying performance can be seen in Tables~\ref{tab:stft_calib_results},
~\ref{tab:mfcc_calib_results} and~\ref{tab:lpc_calib_results}.

of the 72-hour audio file used for the evaluation stage of this project. This implies a possible link between model performance 
and the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the section of audio considered. The increase in FPs also seems to correlate 
with the increase in labelled calls. These observations can be seen in Figure~\ref{fig:5_time_fp}.

Considering the good performance of the model as well as the unlabelled 
TP calls, the STFT feature extraction is the best method to use for call detection with 
the DTW algorithm, and the algorithm is a valid choice of detection method for Bryde's whales within the False Bay region.
