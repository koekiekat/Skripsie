The calibration variables chosen for both the 5 template experiment and 
the 10 template experiment, as shown in Table \ref{tab:stft_calib_results}, 
stayed relatively consistent. The frame length for both experiments was chosen 
to be 0.512 seconds, this frame length was chosen based on the F1 scores 
for the chosen thresholds. The average F1 scores for 5 templates and 10 templates 
was 0.682 and 0.683 respectively. This shows that not only was the frame length 
consistent, the threshold reliability also remianed consistent even if the 
individual threshold scores per call type varied slightly between experiments. 
The chosen vote fraction was not chosen to be the same with a choice of 0.4 
and 0.3 for 5 and 10 templates respectively. This is not, however a 
large difference considering 0.3 would never have been an option for 5 templates 
since 5 multiplied by 0.3 is not a whole number. The overall effect of 
increasing the number of templates for the STFT feature extraction method 
during the calibration stage was not large. It can be considered however that 
the 10 template experiment performed slightly better due to the slightly larger 
average F1 score.

For both STFT experiments the final testing results performed relatively well. 
When considering the calibrated precision and recall points as well as the F1 score, 
as shown in Table \ref{tab:stft_test_results}, it can be seen that the 5 template experiment performed slightly better in terms 
of calibration of thresholds. When consider the average precision one can see that 
the overall model of the 10 template experiment performed better. The PR curves shown 
in Figures \ref{fig:stft_5_pr} and \ref{fig:stft_10_pr} back this up with the red 
point being the precision and recall resulting from the calibrated thrsholds.

Considering the low availability of well labeled data to use for the calibration 
stage of this project as well as human error, which was introduced in manual 
selection of calibration and background templates, the varying performance of the 
calibrated thresholds is justified. The lack of data and human error meant that 
a maximum of 10 templates per call type could be used for calibration. It also caused 
a bias in the choices of templates to train on as selecting suitably representative c
templates per call type became difficult. The selection of background templates for 
calibration meant that the class imbalance between calls and background noise 
differed between calibration and testing. The selection of templates and not 
a continuos audio file for calibration also meant that subsequence DTW did not necessarily 
act within the same conditions as testing thus creating another hinderance. 

When looking at printed spectrograms of False Positives detected by the model 
it was found that for both experiments many of these False Positives were actually 
unlabeled calls. This means that the actual precision of the model may be higher 
than the reported amounts. This makes sense as human error was also introduced in 
call labeling, since the known labeled calls were found by previous projects and 
is therefore not necessarily a complete list.

Considering the relitively good performance of the model as well as the unlabeled 
True Positives STFT feature extraction is a good method to use for call detection with 
the DTW algorithm.

The feature frame lengths selected for 5 and 10 templates did not stay consistent with MFCC as they had with STFT. 
Between the three MFCC experiments, no derivative, 1 derivative added and 2 derivatives added, the frame length selected 
for 5 and 10 templates for each of these experiments remained the same 0.384 for 5 templates and 0.256 for 10 
templates. 

The vote fraction chosen for all three experiments for 5 templates was consistently 0.2, but for 10 templates it changed from 
0.2 to 0.3 to 0.5 for no derivatives to 1 derivitive and 2 erivitives respectively. 

Considering the average F1 scores for 5 templates the MFCC with 2 derivatives added performed the best with an F1 of 0.645. 
For 10 templates the MFCC without derivatives performed best with F1 avergae begin 0.553. However, the difference in F1 between 
consecutive experiments differed at most by 0,1. The general trend in chosen thresholds as derivatives were added is that the 
threshold appeared to increase. This was not a hard rule however.

The genral trend is that the MFCC with 2 drivatives method gave the highest F1 scores, with the 5 template 
experiment doing the best in terms of overall F1 score.

In terms of recall the 5 Template experiment consistently out performed the 10 template experiment no matter 
the amount of derivitives added to the feature. The highest recall for the template 5 experiment was 0.945 when no 
derivatives were used. The highest recall for the 10 template experiment was 0.930, also when no derivitives were used. This shows 
that no derivitives consistiently gives better recall performance, however the difference between the two best recalls in terms of 
each other as well as in terms of the second highest for each respective experiment was not by much with the absolute lowest recall 
found to be 0.860 which is all together not bad. 

In terms of precision the 10 template model consistently out performed the 5 templated esperiment. 
The highest precision for the 10 template experiment was 0.354 when 1 derivitive was used in the feature. The highest for 5 templates 
was 0.141 also at 1 derivitive added. This shows that 1 derivitive added consistently imporved on precision. 
Unlike with recall precision was overall not very high with the highest precision being 0.354 and the lowest being 0.063. This shows 
that MFCC method overall detected many False Positives.

When looking at the F1 scores it can be seen that adding the first derivitive drastically increased the performance for 
both 5 and 10 templates, however adding the second derivitive again made the performance decrease slighlly, however not 
as low as with no derivitives. It can be seen that the change in precision had a larger impact on overall performance of the selected 
thresholds since recall stayed relitively consistent and high.

When looking at the average precision for each model it can be seen that the 10 template model consistently performed better overall as 
a model not considering the calibration thresholds. When looking at the PR curves in Figures \ref{fig:mfcc_pr}, \ref{fig:mfcc_d1_pr} and \ref{fig:mfcc_d2_pr} 
it can clearly be seen that the selected threshold rarely is the best point on the PR curve. This again shows the effect of human error due to lack of 
labeled data in the calibration stage of this project.
